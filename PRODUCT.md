# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Dreamlining serves a small social circle running a live, facilitator-led dreamlining event. Participants join from a phone or computer to write a private dreamline, browse the room’s submitted dreams, offer useful feedback, request contact safely, and leave with one concrete monthly scheme. A facilitator/admin runs the room from `/admin`, creates sessions, shares a QR code, controls phases, monitors equitable attention, and moderates reports.

## Product Purpose

Dreamlining replaces the long shared document that made the event awkward and position-biased. It turns a room of ambitions into a clear, generous rhythm: write privately, reveal by choice, make useful contributions, and leave with momentum. The router recommends less-seen dreamlines without assigning reviewers or hiding the rest of the room.

## Brand Personality

Welcoming, communal, lightly playful. The interface should feel like a thoughtful host setting out a table: clear enough to trust, warm enough to invite participation, and lively without becoming noisy.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS dashboards, pastel “AI” styling, sterile productivity software, popularity feeds, leaderboard energy, and decorative gamification. Avoid visual language that makes a vulnerable blocker feel like a performance metric.

## Design Principles

1. **Host the room.** Make the current phase, next useful action, and shared rhythm obvious without turning the event into a control panel.
2. **Make generosity easy.** Put the contribution prompt at the center and keep useful response types close at hand.
3. **Reveal with agency.** Drafts, private feedback, and contact details should communicate their visibility state at the moment it matters.
4. **Fairness stays quiet.** The router should feel like a helpful nudge, never a public ranking or an assignment notice.
5. **Leave with a spark.** The final commitment should feel like a small, achievable launch—not administrative paperwork.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic labels, screen-reader announcements for phase and save state, strong contrast, text zoom, reduced motion, color-blind-safe status cues, touch targets of at least 44px, and layouts that work at phone and desktop widths. Never rely on color alone to communicate coverage, privacy, or phase.
