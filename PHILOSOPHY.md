# Dreamlining philosophy

## The one thing

**No dream gets buried because it was typed lower down.**

Dreamlining turns a room full of private ambitions into a fair, lightweight burst of attention and a concrete next move. The product should feel like a generous friend keeping the room moving—not like a social feed measuring who is popular.

## Principles

1. **Attention is a shared resource.** Every submitted dreamline is available to the room, while personalized recommendations quietly steer attention toward people who have received less feedback.
2. **Agency beats coercion.** The router recommends; it never assigns. Participants can browse, skip, revisit, and comment when they have something real to offer.
3. **Visibility is earned by consent.** Drafts stay private until submission. Comments are public by default, private by explicit choice, and private content reaches a facilitator only when reported.
4. **Privacy is specific, not vague.** Contact information is never a directory. A person approves one requester and one contact method at a time.
5. **Momentum over performance.** The session ends with one achievable monthly scheme and a first action, not a perfect life plan.
6. **The facilitator is a conductor, not a monitor.** Admin controls keep time and recover from exceptions without turning the room into an analytics dashboard.

## Taste boundaries

- No popularity counts, leaderboard, public ranking, or stable document-order feed.
- No accounts, social graph, algorithmic content generation, or persistent public profile in the first release.
- No hidden private values shipped to the browser and no contact-list export.
- No forced reviewer assignment; fairness is achieved through recommendation ordering and coverage measurement.
- No automatic phase transition that can discard someone’s work.
